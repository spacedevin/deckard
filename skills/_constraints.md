# Constraint cards — one rule to play against

The director draws one or two of these for a chapter or a move. A constraint is a creative push, so play it
for real (a listener should be able to hear it), and drop it when the chapter ends. If a card fights the room,
draw again. Each card is `## id` then the rule.

## no-kick-yet
The kick does not arrive until the second scene. Make the intro groove without it.

## bass-sings
The bass plays the melody. The lead, if there is one, only answers it.

## one-chord
The whole chapter stays on one chord. Movement comes from rhythm, timbre and the bass.

## odd-phrase
One part loops every 3 or 5 bars against the 8-bar phrase, so the pattern lines up differently each time.

## call-answer
Two parts trade phrases: one plays, then the other answers in the gap. Never both at once.

## halftime-flip
Halfway through the chapter the drums switch to halftime at the same tempo, then come back.

## borrowed-voice
Use one instrument in a job it is not usually given: a bell as the bass, a pad as percussion, a vocal as a pad.

## subtract-to-drop
The biggest moment in the chapter has fewer parts than the scene before it.

## space-bar
Every 8 bars, one full bar of near silence (only a texture or an echo tail).

## steal-the-hook
Reuse the previous chapter's hook, reharmonised or on a different instrument, so the set has a thread.

## velocity-only
The groove changes only through accents and ghosts (step_vel, step_prob) for one whole scene. Same notes.

## filter-story
One filter ride across the whole chapter (16–32 bars) is the main event. Plan the arc of the cutoff.

## key-lift
The final scene of the chapter is a whole step up from where it started.

## three-notes
The hook uses only three pitches. Rhythm does the rest.

## dry-wet
Alternate scenes between completely dry and drenched in reverb and delay.

## new-pocket
Move the hat or the clap off its usual step (late offbeats, a skipped 2 and 4) so the pocket feels new.

## low-only-intro
The intro is only low end: kick, sub, maybe a muffled pad. Everything above 1 kHz arrives later.

## ghost-drums
The drums play at low velocity with probability; the bass and chords carry the energy.

## tempo-travel
This chapter sits at a clearly different tempo than the last one (at least 8 bpm away, or a halftime feel).

## answer-the-room
Build the chapter around something another co-DJ just played: echo their rhythm or answer their melody.

## arp-engine
One arpeggiated part (voice arp) drives the chapter; change its rate or octave instead of adding parts.

## two-tempo-feel
Hats in triplets over a straight kick for at least one scene.

## sparse-hook
The hook plays once every two bars with long rests. Make every note count.

## swap-roles
Halfway through, the chord part becomes the rhythm (short stabs) and the drums thin out to carry less.

## modal-color
Use a mode for colour (dorian lift, phrygian darkness, lydian brightness) and make the colour note audible.

## long-intro
Take 16 bars to bring the groove in. One element per 4 bars.

## break-the-loop
Once a scene has played twice, it must change one clip number before it plays again.

## minimal-palette
At most four parts in the whole chapter. Make each one carry more.

## ear-candy
One small surprise every 16 bars: a ratchet roll, a pitch drop, a throw, a reversed-feel swell.

## warm-down
End the chapter softer than it started: filter closing, parts dropping out, tempo held, energy falling.
