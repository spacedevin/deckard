# Genre idioms — what makes a style feel like itself

Techniques, not presets. Each style is a few habits a listener recognises. Combine habits from two styles for
a hybrid. Every style has a starter kit in `codj.library('kit')` (`pack_kit_<style>`); these notes are about
what to do with it.

## house
Four-to-the-floor kick, offbeat open hat, clap on 2 and 4. Groove lives in the shuffle (nudge the 16ths late) and
in a piano or organ stab (tine, pad with short release) on the offbeats. Basslines bounce on the offbeat and
leave the downbeat to the kick. 118–126 bpm. Breakdowns strip to chords and a vocal-like lead.

## deep-house
House with the lights down: minor 7th and 9th chords on a warm pad or tine, a rounded sub bass, softer hats with
probability, long reverb. Energy is a slow tide. 118–122 bpm.

## techno
Hypnotic repetition. A driving kick, a rumble or offbeat bass, metallic percussion, one modulating synth line.
The arrangement is filter and timbre: ride `fx/cutoff`, `fx/res`, `gen/decay` over 16–32 bars. Few chords, if any.
126–138 bpm.

## dub-techno
Techno with chords smeared into echoes: one minor chord stab, heavy delay and reverb throws, a deep sub, hats
very soft. Almost no melody. The echo is the hook. 118–126 bpm.

## acid
A resonant 16th-note line (`acid303`) with accents and slides is the whole song. Ride cutoff and resonance;
change the accent pattern by clip number. Keep the drums simple. 124–132 bpm.

## trance
Rolling offbeat bass, a long build with a riser and a snare roll, a big supersaw-like lead (syncLead, obSync,
detuned pad) that plays a soaring 8-bar melody. Breakdowns are long and melodic. 136–140 bpm.

## dnb
Breakbeat drums at 170–176 bpm (kick on 1, snare on 2 and 4 of a halftime-feel bar, busy ghost hats), a reese
or sub bass with movement. Rollers keep one bass phrase; liquid adds pads and a soft melody.

## breakbeat
Syncopated broken kicks and snares at 125–135, funky bass, stabs. Swing matters. Fills with ratchets.

## trap
Halftime feel at 130–150 bpm: sparse kick and an 808 that slides, a snare or clap on beat 3, rolling hat
triplets and ratchets, dark minor melodies on a bell or pluck. Lots of space.

## lofi
70–90 bpm, swung and slightly late drums, soft velocities, jazz chords (min9, maj7) on tine or halo, a round bass,
crackle-like noise. Everything low-passed. Short loops that change chord rather than add parts.

## garage
2-step: the kick skips (not four-to-the-floor), shuffled hats, snares on 2 and 4 with ghosts around them, chopped
vocal-like leads, warm organ chords. 130–134 bpm.

## ambient
No kick needed. Long pads, drones, slow chord changes, bell or tine motifs with long tails, texture moving over
minutes. Energy is low and rising slowly. Tempo is a suggestion.

## latin
Clave-led percussion (3-2 or 2-3), congas and bells as patterns on drumSynth and bell, piano montuno-like
offbeat stabs, a tumbao bass that anticipates the downbeat. 100–125 bpm.

## funk
The one: everything lands on the downbeat of the bar. Syncopated 16th bass with rests, clean guitar or keys
scratching 16ths, tight drums with ghost snares. 95–115 bpm.

## chiptune
Square and pulse leads (chiptune, nes2a03, gameBoyDmg), fast arpeggios for chords, noise-channel drums, bright and
major or heroic minor. 130–160 bpm.

## synthwave
Gated-sounding snare on 2 and 4, straight 8th bass arpeggio, lush pads and a bright lead with long glide,
nostalgic major/minor progressions. 85–118 bpm.
